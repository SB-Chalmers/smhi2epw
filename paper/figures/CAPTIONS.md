# Figure captions (round 2)

Draft captions for the redrawn figures. The first sentence of each caption states the finding and matches the panel titles. Figure numbers follow the manuscript, and file names are in brackets.

House rules for every figure: Stepwise is drawn in the one accent colour (purple) and every other method is grey. Lines and series are labelled on the figure, not in legends. Method names are those of Table 6. Season colours are those of Figure 4.

**Figure 1 (fig_a_recovery_ladder).** Stepwise fills short gaps from measurements and switches to ERA5 once a gap is longer than two days (general weather) or three hours (solar radiation). Each bar shows the gap lengths a source may fill. Sources are tried from the top, and an hour one source cannot fill falls to the next. A nearby station is used only if it passes the test in Section 3.3. The light bar is ERA5 filling any hour still empty. The grey dashed bars are used only without the switch (Stepwise, no switch). The switch at one day for general weather and at 12 h and 48 h for solar were also tested (Section 6.6). Solar radiation is never taken from a nearby station. Where a solar station lies within 50 km, its measured total is used and STRÅNG's direct and diffuse split is scaled to it.

**Figure 2 (fig_d_workflow).** Every filling method fills the same hidden hours in the same reference years, so each test file differs from its reference only in the filled gaps. The eight methods are those of Table 6.

**Figure 3 (fig_b_map).** The six study regions and the stations behind each reference year. Lines join each main station to its nearby station (and solar station). In g the years label the lines, because the main station changes from year to year. † Kiruna 2018 had no station that met the data requirements.

**Figure 4 (fig_c_masking).** Gaps are placed once per season in three random draws, or once per file at a weather event. Gaps of 3 h and 12 h are drawn wider than to scale so they stay visible. General-weather and solar gaps are hidden in separate files. Winter is December to February. Event hours (b) are the warmest and the coldest measured hour, the hottest hour of a heatwave and the coldest hour of a cold spell, where these occur.

**Figure 5 (fig06_annual_error).** Annual heating demand stays within 2 % even when a week of general weather is filled, but a hidden heatwave week can move annual cooling by 6 % (general weather) or 20 % (solar radiation). Stepwise; 95th percentile of the absolute error over 17 region-years × 6 buildings. Filled dots: random gaps, one per season. Open dots: one gap per file, centred on the coldest hour of a cold spell (heating) or the hottest hour of a heatwave (cooling), the same selection as in Figure 8. The y-axes differ between panels.

**Figure 6 (fig09_peaks).** Annual totals hold, but peaks move: at one week, half of the heating peaks hidden by a gap at a weather event move by more than a day. a, b: when the yearly peak hour fell inside a gap at a weather event, whether the filled file puts the peak in the same hour, within a day, or more than a day away (% of cases). c, d: peak load error for each building case whose peak fell in a gap (grey: random gaps; purple: gaps at events). Solid bars are medians and dashed bars 95th percentiles, drawn where there are at least 10 cases. Stepwise, general weather.

**Figure 7 (fig10_events).** Stepwise keeps most weather events intact even when a whole week is hidden, while a straight line loses most of them: 60 % of heatwaves are kept against 7 % with Straight line only. Each bar shows the events hit by a gap centred on the event that are kept (the same start and end), changed (shorter, longer or shifted) or lost (no longer an event). Heatwave: three or more days at or above 25 °C. Warm spell: three or more days above the summer 95th percentile. Cold spell: three or more days at or below −10 °C. Relative cold spell: three or more days below the winter 5th percentile. General weather.

**Figure 8 (fig08_gap_position).** Gaps in summer and at heatwaves carry most of the error, and a hidden heatwave week dominates for solar radiation. Stepwise. Dot area grows with the 95th percentile of the load error over the gap and the following week, as % of the building's annual demand (the number beside each dot). Ringed dots are gaps centred on a cold spell (heating) or a heatwave (cooling), as in Figure 5. Colours are the seasons of Figure 4. Dot sizes are scaled within each panel.

**Figure 9 (fig07_methods).** At 48 h Stepwise is among the lowest errors for every case; at one week Stepwise, ERA5 only and diyepw-style give the same result, because all three use ERA5 there, and only the straight line fails badly. Open dots: 48-hour gaps. Filled dots and numbers: one-week gaps. Methods are ranked by the one-week value. Random gaps, 17 region-years × 6 buildings. a, b: all general-weather gaps. c, d: only the gaps where a nearby station passed the test (about 40 %), so Nearby station can be compared on the same cases. e, f: solar gaps, which have no nearby-station step. Values beyond the axis are shown by an arrow and their number. Line then patterns is not shown, because it does not fill gaps longer than 48 h; up to 48 h it equals Stepwise, patterns first (general weather) or Stepwise, no switch (solar).

**Figure 11 (fig11_monthly).** With Stepwise, few months fall outside ±5 % for heating, but at one week about one month in five does for cooling; a straight line fails in up to 80 % of months. Share of months with a gap whose demand falls outside ±5 % of the reference, the monthly bias limit for calibrated models in ASHRAE Guideline 14. Months with little demand are left out. Dots, rows and ranking as in Figure 9, on a 0–60 % axis. Line then patterns is not shown, because it has no one-week result.
